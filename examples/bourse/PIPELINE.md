# Bourse — The Full Product Pipeline
## How "Bourse" would be built inside a AAA company — idea to GA to sunset

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Version | 1.0 |
| Date | 29 August 2026 |
| Companion doc | BOURSE-PRD-v1.0.md |
| Purpose | Reference model for the complete lifecycle, the formal gates, the artifacts produced at each stage, which parts transfer to a lean build, and the exact execution order for an agentic coding workflow |

**Note on accuracy:** the process shapes below reflect widely-documented practice at large product organisations. Specific tool and review-body names change; the *shape* — the gates, the artifacts, the sign-off structure — is stable and is the useful part.

**Note on adaptation:** Bourse differs from a UGC social product in three structurally important ways, and this changes the pipeline materially:

1. **No user-generated content.** The Trust & Safety workstream — typically 20–30% of a UGC launch — largely collapses. What replaces it is *generated*-content review: the LLM narrative pipeline needs a quality and safety gate, but it is an offline batch process with a human reviewer, not a 24/7 abuse-response operation.
2. **The core risk is fidelity, not scale.** Frame's product-killing risk was whether an aesthetic model could beat baseline. Bourse's is whether a synthetic order book reproduces real market statistics. Both are single technical bets that must be tested in month one.
3. **The regulatory surface is financial, not platform.** No DSA, no DMCA, no CSAM. Instead: financial-services app-store policy, investment-advice regulation, gambling classification risk, and minors' data.

## Phase 0 — Origination

**How this idea enters the system**

| Path | Applicability to Bourse |
|---|---|
| Top-down strategy | Plausible inside a broker or exchange: "our funnel dies at first-time-investor education" |
| Bottom-up / discretionary time | Very plausible — a simulator prototype is a satisfying engineering side project, and the engine is the kind of thing an engineer builds for its own sake |
| Incubator / Labs | Most likely home. A consumer education product with no revenue model needs incubation, not a P&L |
| Acquisition | Common in this category — brokers buy education products rather than build them |
| Regulatory adjacency | A broker building investor-education tooling to satisfy regulatory expectations around suitability and financial literacy |

**Artifacts produced**
- A **one-pager**: problem, user, why now, why us, size of prize. Two pages maximum.
- A scrappy demo. For this product specifically, the demo should be a **price chart generated by a working order book** — not a mockup. A tape that looks real is worth more than any deck, because it is the only part of the pitch that is hard.

**The real gate is social, not formal:** someone senior must put their name on it.

**Bourse-specific pitch framing inside a large company:** *"the funnel from curious to confident investor is broken, and every existing paper-trading product is a price feed with a buy button — it teaches nothing about slippage, settlement, tax drag, or what a bear market feels like. A real simulation of a fake market is the only way to teach those, and it's a category with no credible incumbent."*

The counter-argument that kills it inside a big company: **no revenue model and a small addressable market.** Which is, as with Frame, precisely why it is a better independent build.

## Phase 1 — Opportunity assessment

**Who's involved:** PM (lead), UXR partner, Strategy & Ops analyst, eng lead, Finance.

**Work performed**

1. **User sizing** — not TAM. How many people plausibly want to learn markets through simulation rather than through video content or by losing real money? The honest number is in the low millions globally, not hundreds of millions. State it plainly; a strategy doc that inflates this loses credibility with the council.
2. **Competitive teardown** — real-money brokers with paper modes (Zerodha Varsity + Kite paper, Thinkorswim paperMoney, Webull paper, TradingView replay), standalone sims (Investopedia Simulator, Wall Street Survivor, Stock Trainer), games (Stock Market Simulator, Wall Street Raider, Capitalism Lab), and education-first products (Varsity, Khan Academy finance). The finding that matters: **almost every one of them fakes the microstructure.** Fills are instant at last price, there is no book, no borrow, no settlement, and usually no tax. That gap is the entire product thesis and the teardown must prove it exists.
3. **Strategic fit** — for a broker, does it feed account opening? For a standalone, is there a defensible asset? (Answer: the engine and the Codex corpus.)
4. **Build / buy / partner** — formal recommendation.
5. **Cost model** — compute-dominated. A server-authoritative shared world running a 250ms tick across 500+ instruments with synthetic participants is a persistent, non-elastic compute cost that scales with *world size*, not user count — which is unusual and worth modelling carefully. Storage is trivial by comparison. LLM narrative generation is a batch cost, amortised.
6. **Cannibalisation** — inside a broker, does a good simulator delay real account funding? (Probably it accelerates it, but the question will be asked.)

**Artifact:** strategy doc (10–20 pages) plus review deck.

**Gate: Product Council review.** Fund, fund-to-milestone, kill, or merge. Most ideas die here.

**Lean translation:** a founder weekend and a spreadsheet. **Do the competitive teardown properly anyway** — you need to know, concretely, which competitor fakes which mechanic, because that list becomes your differentiation copy and your test matrix.

## Phase 3 — Discovery, foundational research, and the feasibility spike

This phase contains the single most important gate in the entire pipeline.

### 3.1 The Engine Feasibility Spike — the gate in disguise

**This is Bourse's equivalent of Frame's ML feasibility spike, and it must be run in month one, before anything else is staffed.**

Build a minimal Rust limit order book with a synthetic participant population. Run it for the equivalent of ten simulated years across a handful of instruments. Then test whether the output is statistically indistinguishable from real market data on the **stylised facts of asset returns** — the empirical regularities that every real market exhibits and that naive random-walk generators fail:

| Stylised fact | Test | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Fat tails** | Kurtosis of returns; tail index estimation | Excess kurtosis well above 0; tail index in the 2–5 range |
| **Absence of linear autocorrelation** | Autocorrelation of raw returns at lags 1–20 | Statistically indistinguishable from zero beyond lag 1 |
| **Volatility clustering** | Autocorrelation of *absolute* or squared returns | Significant, slowly decaying positive autocorrelation |
| **Aggregational Gaussianity** | Distribution shape as sampling interval increases | Converges toward normal at longer horizons |
| **Leverage effect** | Correlation between returns and future volatility | Negative |
| **Volume–volatility correlation** | Correlation of volume and absolute returns | Strongly positive |
| **Order book shape** | Depth profile away from mid | Humped, not flat or monotonic |
| **Spread–volatility relationship** | Spread as a function of realised volatility | Positive, widening in stress |
| **Trade sign autocorrelation** | Autocorrelation of buy/sell initiator sign | Persistent positive (order splitting signature) |
| **Impact function shape** | Price impact vs. order size | Concave, approximately square-root |

**If the engine cannot produce these, the product thesis is dead** — because "very very very accurate market behaviour" is the entire promise, and a simulation that fails these tests is a random walk wearing a costume. Kill here, cheaply, for the cost of one or two engineers and six weeks, rather than after fifty people are staffed against it.

**This spike's output — the test harness itself — becomes permanent CI infrastructure.** Every subsequent engine change re-runs it. This is the single highest-value artifact produced in the entire project.

### 3.2 UX Research program

| Study type | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational / generative | 20–30 interviews across all four personas: absolute beginners, active retail investors, students, and simulation-game players | Problem validation, persona set, jobs-to-be-done |
| Workflow mapping | How does a beginner currently try to learn? Where do they give up? | The abandonment map — reveals where teaching must be inserted |
| Failed-learner interviews | People who *tried* to learn investing and stopped | Higher signal than successful investors. This is the study most likely to be skipped and most likely to be decisive. |
| Concept testing | 3–4 divergent framings: simulator? game? course? career sim? | Which framing resonates per persona |
| Competitive usability | Watch users on Investopedia Simulator and a broker paper-trading mode | Friction inventory, and confirmation that fake microstructure is *noticed* |
| Quantitative survey | n=2,000+ | Segment sizing, prior experience distribution, tolerance for realism vs. approachability |

**The critical research question for Bourse:** *does realism attract or repel the beginner?* The PRD bets that realism attracts, with progressive disclosure managing the load. That bet must be tested with concept work in Phase 3, not discovered in beta.

### 3.3 Data science in parallel

Define the metrics framework via **Goals → Signals → Metrics**. For Bourse this is where the bust-rate target band (8–15% in 30 days) gets derived and stress-tested rather than asserted — it is a *balance* parameter disguised as a metric, and getting it wrong in either direction breaks the product.

**Artifacts:** research readout, persona set, journey maps, concept test results, **engine feasibility memo with the stylised-facts test results**, metrics framework doc.

## Phase 4 — Product definition

**The PRD** — the companion document. Inside a large company it would additionally carry:

- Explicit **OKRs** with numeric targets for the launch quarter
- A **launch tier** proposal
- **Non-goals** stated aggressively (already present — §3.2)
- **Kill criteria**: the thresholds at which the product is sunset. See §4.2 below.

### 4.1 The Simulation Fidelity Specification — the highest-leverage document

**This is Bourse's equivalent of Frame's rater guidelines, and it deserves the same disproportionate seriousness.**

Frame's problem was encoding *taste* so that a contract rater in another country could apply it consistently. Bourse's problem is encoding *realism* so that an engineer with no market background can implement it correctly and a test suite can verify it.

The Fidelity Spec defines, in operational and testable language:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Microstructure invariants** | The stylised-facts table from §3.1, as permanent acceptance criteria with numeric bounds |
| **Participant behavioural specification** | For each of the seven archetypes: fair-value estimation method, noise distribution, order sizing rule, order placement logic, cancellation logic, inventory management, stress behaviour. Precise enough to implement without interpretation. |
| **Instrument liquidity profiles** | Participant population mix per market-cap tier and asset class, and the resulting expected spread, depth, and impact for each |
| **Corporate action mechanics** | Exact cash, position, and cost-basis treatment for all fourteen action types, with worked examples |
| **Cost and tax rule sets** | MSX and ATX, itemised, with worked examples per instrument type and holding period. Reviewed by the domain expert. |
| **Settlement and margin rules** | T+1 mechanics, intraday reuse, borrow availability and fees, buy-in triggers, margin computation, call and liquidation sequence |
| **Macro transmission specification** | How a policy rate change propagates to discount rates, bond prices, FX, sector betas, and credit spreads — with magnitudes |
| **Regime and shock parameterisation** | State definitions, transition probabilities, correlation effects, recovery shapes |
| **Fidelity exceptions register** | Every place the sim knowingly deviates from reality, with the justification and the reason it does not compromise learning transfer |

That last section is what separates an honest document from a marketing one. Stretched trading hours is a deviation. Compressed time is a deviation. They belong in the register with their rationale, so that no engineer ever "fixes" them and no reviewer ever mistakes them for bugs.

**This document is the simulation, encoded.** Underestimating it is the classic failure mode — the equivalent of Frame shipping an aesthetic model with no written definition of aesthetic quality.

### 4.2 Kill criteria — written now, not later

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Engine fidelity | Fails three or more stylised-facts tests after two remediation cycles |
| Beginner accessibility | < 40% of Persona A test users complete a trade in onboarding after two design iterations |
| Retention | D30 below 8% after two iteration cycles post-beta |
| Bust-rate balance | Cannot be tuned into the 8–15% band without removing permanence |
| Cost | Compute cost per active user exceeds the sustainable ceiling at projected scale |
| Content | Codex cannot reach 400 glossary entries at acceptable quality within the content budget |

Stating these upfront is what prevents zombie products.

### 4.3 Parallel definition artifacts

- **UX design brief and design principles** (PRD §5, §14.5)
- **Content standards doc** — voice, reading level, and the absolute constraint that no Codex entry, tip, or coaching card may be phrased as a recommendation
- **Narrative generation guidelines** — the constrained-generation rules for company profiles and editorial news: consistency with the parameter vector, prohibition on resemblance to real companies or persons, tone, and the human review protocol
- **Data governance doc** — collected data, retention, deletion propagation, export format

**Gate:** PRD approved and signed by eng, design, and sponsor. Fidelity Spec LGTM'd by the microstructure specialist. OKRs and kill criteria locked.

## Phase 5 — Technical design

**Engineering design docs.** Circulated, commented on by senior engineers outside the team, revised until they earn LGTM. Bourse needs roughly:

| Design doc | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Matching engine** | Book data structure, price-time priority, tick bands, auction algorithms, circuit breakers, halt handling, determinism and replay |
| **Participant simulation** | Archetype implementations, population management, fair-value distribution, per-instrument scheduling, compute budget per tick |
| **Fundamental model** | Statement generation, sector demand cycles, DCF fair value, consensus formation, surprise mechanics |
| **Macro engine** | State evolution, central bank reaction function, yield curve construction, transmission to instrument pricing |
| **Corporate actions service** | Event scheduling, position and basis adjustment, cash movement, notification fan-out |
| **Tick loop & scheduling** | 250ms budget allocation across 500+ instruments, backpressure, degradation strategy under load |
| **Time-series storage** | Candle aggregation, retention tiering, query patterns, TimescaleDB hypertable design |
| **Account & ledger** | Double-entry ledger for cash, positions, fees, taxes; settlement state machine; audit guarantees |
| **Margin & risk service** | Real-time margin computation, call detection, liquidation sequencing |
| **Order gateway** | Validation, eligibility enforcement, rate limiting, idempotency |
| **Client architecture** | RN + Electron shared core, native module FFI boundary, local replica and reconciliation, offline behaviour |
| **Sync & Away Summary** | Delta computation, compaction, generation latency budget |
| **Content pipeline** | Company genesis, LLM generation, validation, human review workflow, publication |
| **Experimentation** | Layer design, metric instrumentation, guardrails |
| **Privacy design document** | Data flows, retention, deletion, export |

**Cross-cutting reviews initiated here (all run for months):**

- **Security review** — the order gateway is the attack surface; a client that can forge fills destroys the shared world and the future leaderboards
- **Privacy review** — light relative to a photo product, but minors and export are real
- **Responsible AI review** — narrower than Frame's, but present. Two concerns: **(a)** LLM-generated narrative must not defame, must not resemble real companies or persons, and must not generate content that reads as real financial information; **(b)** generated coaching must never cross from explanation into recommendation. Required artifacts: generation constraints, human review protocol, and a red-team pass on prompt injection through any user-supplied field.
- **Accessibility review** — a numerically dense, colour-coded product has a real accessibility burden. Screen-reader ordering of financial values and the colourblind P&L strategy (PRD §14.7) are design work, not a checklist item.
- **Internationalisation review** — English at v1, but lakh/crore vs. thousands grouping and currency formatting are jurisdiction-dependent from day one and must not be retrofitted.

**Gate:** all design docs LGTM'd; SRE agrees an engagement model; the tick-loop compute budget is approved against the cost model.

## Phase 6 — Build & internal iteration

**Engineering practice**
- Trunk-based development, every change code-reviewed
- Automated presubmit testing; no merge without green — **including the stylised-facts suite**
- Feature flags on everything; nothing ships without a kill switch
- Continuous integration into an internal build the team uses daily

### The internal release ladder

| Stage | Audience | Purpose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fishfood** | The team, ~50 people | Does it hold together | Weeks 1–8 |
| **Dogfood** | Company-wide volunteers | Real usage, real bugs | 2–4 months |
| **Trusted Tester** | External NDA group: **experienced traders and market professionals** | The only group who can tell you the simulation is wrong | 2–3 months |
| **Closed beta** | Invited external users, weighted toward beginners | Onboarding, balance, bust rate | 2–3 months |
| **Open beta** | Public, labelled beta | Load, retention curves, long-horizon balance | 3–6 months |

**The dogfood trap, Bourse edition:** internal employees are disproportionately technical and will over-index on engine correctness and feature depth while under-indexing on whether a beginner survives onboarding. **Two distinct external stages are therefore mandatory and non-substitutable:** experienced traders to validate fidelity, and genuine beginners to validate approachability. A single blended beta will tell you neither.

**Running in parallel through Phase 6**
- **Evaluative UXR** — usability rounds every 2–3 weeks
- **Fidelity regression** — the stylised-facts suite runs on every build; drift is treated as a P0 bug, not a tuning matter
- **Balance tuning** — bust rate, skill point rate, stipend size, difficulty multipliers. This is game-design work and needs dedicated ownership, not PM spare time.
- **Content production** — 600 glossary entries, 80 guides, 30 playbooks. At sustainable quality this is 8–10 months of specialist work and is chronically under-planned. Start it in Phase 5.
- **Long-horizon soak testing** — run the world continuously for months of wall-clock time before launch. Compounding drift, unbounded state growth, and degenerate market conditions only appear over simulated years. **A simulator that has never been run for six months has never been tested.**
- **Load and chaos testing** — including engine node failure mid-session and recovery to a consistent book

## Phase 7 — Compliance & launch review

Every required reviewer is a blocking item. Nothing ships with an open blocker.

| Review | Owner | What they block on |
|---|---|---|
| Legal — product counsel | Legal | ToS, disclaimers, liability limitation |
| Legal — IP/trademark | Legal | Product name clearance; **clearance that no generated fictional company resembles a real entity** |
| **Financial services regulatory** | Compliance | **The critical one.** Confirmation the product does not constitute investment advice (SEBI Investment Adviser / Research Analyst regulations in India; SEC/FINRA in the US), is not an unregistered trading platform, and carries adequate simulated-performance disclaimers |
| **Gambling classification** | Legal | Assessment that fictional-market trading with no real-money stake and no cash-out is not gambling in target jurisdictions. **The absence of any coin purchase mechanism in v1 is materially helpful here and should be understood as a compliance decision, not only a product one.** Any future IAP triggers full re-review. |
| App store policy | Compliance | Google Play financial-services policy: simulated-trading apps require clear labelling and must not imply real trading. Microsoft Store equivalent. |
| Minors | Privacy + Legal | Age gate, parental consent under India's DPDP Act and COPPA, and a judgement on whether a realistic trading simulator is appropriate for under-13s at all |
| Privacy | Privacy Eng + counsel | PDD approved, deletion works end-to-end, export functional |
| Regulatory — data | Compliance | GDPR, DPDP Act, US state privacy laws |
| Security | Security Eng | Pen test clean; order gateway hardened |
| Responsible AI | RAI review | Generation constraints, review protocol, no-advice guarantee, defamation and real-entity resemblance controls |
| Accessibility | a11y | Meets the bar including colourblind P&L and screen-reader numeric ordering |
| i18n | i18n | Locale-aware number and currency formatting correct per jurisdiction |
| SRE — **Production Readiness Review** | SRE | SLOs defined, monitoring complete, runbooks written, on-call staffed, load tested, rollback tested. **Additionally: engine determinism and world-state recovery must be demonstrated** — a shared authoritative world that cannot be recovered after a node loss is an unshippable failure mode. |
| Data governance | Data | Retention and deletion enforced systemically |
| Support readiness | Support | Help centre, escalation trained |
| Comms / PR | Comms | Messaging approved; negative-scenario Q&A prepared |
| Marketing | PMM | Store listings, assets, campaign |
| Executive sign-off | VP/SVP | Final go |

**Duration:** 3–9 months in parallel with build.

**Bourse-specific hard spots:** the financial-advice determination, gambling classification across jurisdictions, and the app-store financial-services policy. Any one can add a quarter. **The single most common cause of launch slip is starting these late** — and for this product they must start in Phase 5, because an adverse gambling determination in a target market is a product-shape problem, not a copy-change problem.

## Phase 8 — Staged rollout & experimentation

**Nothing launches at 100%.**

1. **1% in one market** — validate instrumentation, check guardrails
2. **5% → 10%** — power to read primary metrics
3. **Holdback** — a permanent holdback retained so long-run impact stays measurable
4. **Geographic staging** — home jurisdiction first, then the second
5. **50% → 100%**

**A structural note unique to Bourse:** because the world is shared and server-authoritative, user-facing experiments must be **client-side or account-side only**. You cannot A/B test the market itself without forking the world, which would break the shared-world property that v2 leaderboards depend on. Any experiment touching engine parameters requires a separate world shard and must be designed as such from the start.

**The experiments that actually matter:**
- Difficulty preset parameters, especially starting capital and volatility multiplier
- Bust-rate calibration — the central balance question
- Onboarding length: four minutes to first trade vs. a longer guided path
- JIT tip frequency and its effect on both retention and decision quality
- Skill point earn rate and tree pacing
- Stipend and salary magnitude — do inflows undermine stakes
- Away Summary format and its effect on return-session length
- Default dashboard composition per difficulty

**Guardrails that would block a launch:**
- **Fidelity regression** — the stylised-facts suite must stay green in production
- Bust rate outside 4–25% (outside the tunable band entirely)
- Median time-to-first-trade above 8 minutes
- Codex open rate below 40% (indicates the reference layer is undiscoverable)
- Compute cost per active user above ceiling
- Crash-free rate below 99%

**Gate:** primary metrics clear the pre-agreed GA bar; no guardrail regressed.

## Phase 9 — General availability

- Launch tier determines the moment
- App store submissions and review — **budget extra time; financial-simulation apps draw additional policy scrutiny and rejection on first submission is common**
- Press embargo, briefings, early access for finance educators and creators
- Support surge staffing for 30 days
- **War room** for 72 hours with SRE and engine engineers on rotation — the failure mode to watch is world-state corruption under load, which is unrecoverable if not caught immediately
- Publish the fidelity position publicly: what is simulated, what is deviated, and why. For this product, transparency about the simulation *is* marketing.

## Phase 10 — Operate, iterate, or sunset

**Ongoing rituals**
- Weekly metrics review; monthly business review; quarterly OKR grading
- **Blameless postmortems** for every incident, published internally, action items tracked
- **Continuous fidelity regression** on every engine release, with the stylised-facts report published to the team like a test result, because that is what it is
- Balance review each cycle: bust rate, progression pacing, inflow magnitude
- Content expansion: Codex entries, new playbooks, new company cohorts entering via IPO
- Annual portfolio review against the kill criteria

**The sunset process** — a formal pipeline of its own: deprecation announcement, data export tooling, wind-down period, final shutdown.

**Bourse's PRD must specify the export commitment now**, and for a reason sharper than Frame's. The product's core promise is a **permanent, never-resetting career record**. A user who is asked to invest months into a portfolio that cannot survive the product's death has been sold a promise the company cannot keep. The Vault export (PRD §13.4) is not a compliance feature — it is the thing that makes permanence credible in the first place. Ship it at v1, not at sunset.

## What this means for a lean build

Fifty people, twenty-four months, and twenty blocking reviewers are not available. The value of the map is knowing **which gates carry real risk** and which are organisational overhead.

### Keep — these prevent product-killing failures

| Gate | Why it's non-negotiable for Bourse |
|---|---|
| **Engine feasibility spike, month one** | If the book can't reproduce fat tails, volatility clustering, and concave impact, the entire product thesis is dead. Six weeks and one engineer to find out. Everything else is wasted if this fails. |
| **Stylised-facts suite as permanent CI** | Fidelity silently degrades under optimisation pressure. Without an automated guard, the sim becomes a random walk over eighteen months of well-intentioned commits and nobody notices. |
| **Simulation Fidelity Specification** | This is how realism becomes a system rather than one person's memory of how markets work. Highest-leverage document in the build; no substitute. |
| **Fidelity exceptions register** | Prevents both accidental "fixes" of deliberate deviations and dishonest marketing claims. |
| **Foundational UXR — especially failed learners** | 20 interviews. Cheap. The alternative is building for an imagined beginner. |
| **Two separate external test groups** | Experienced traders for fidelity, genuine beginners for approachability. Blending them tells you nothing. |
| **Long-horizon soak test** | Run the world for months of wall-clock time before launch. Drift, unbounded growth, and degenerate states are invisible at short horizons. |
| **Financial-advice and gambling determinations, early** | An adverse finding is a product-shape problem, not a copy problem. Get counsel in Phase 5, not Phase 7. |
| **No-advice constraint enforced in code** | Every tip, coaching card, and Codex entry passes a review rule: explains, never recommends. This is both a legal and a product-integrity requirement. |
| **Vault export at v1** | The permanence promise is not credible without it. |
| **Kill criteria, written down** | Decide now what "this isn't working" looks like. |
| **Staged rollout behind flags** | Even at small scale. |
| **World-state recovery test** | A shared authoritative world that can't be recovered after a crash is unshippable. Test the restore, not just the backup. |

### Compress

Design docs (write them, but three pages not thirty) · one design sprint, not four · localisation (English at launch, but keep number formatting jurisdiction-aware from day one — retrofitting lakh/crore is miserable) · accessibility (do colourblind P&L and screen-reader numeric ordering; defer the full audit) · formal PRR (write runbooks and set SLOs; skip the ceremony) · content (launch with 400 glossary entries and 40 guides; expand post-launch, but ship every playbook's failure section — a playbook without one is worse than no playbook).

### Skip

Product Council review · headcount planning cycles · dogfood as a distinct stage · blocking review tooling · Tier 1 comms strategy · quarterly OKR grading rituals · cannibalisation analysis · PMM function.

### The asymmetry worth naming

A large company's pipeline is optimised to avoid catastrophic failure at enormous scale. It is structurally poor at products like this one, because the process cost is fixed while the prize is modest and the required expertise is unusually deep and narrow. **Bourse is a better independent build than a corporate one** — and that is exactly why the four gates that *do* transfer must be run with disproportionate seriousness. The engine spike, the fidelity suite, the Fidelity Spec, and the no-advice constraint are the ones where a small team's shortcuts produce failures identical in kind to a large team's, and the failures are not recoverable late.

## Appendix A — Execution order for an agentic coding workflow

This appendix translates the pipeline into an order of operations for an AI coding agent, with an explicit definition of done and a stop condition per stage. **Stages are strictly ordered. Do not begin a stage until the prior stage's exit criteria are demonstrably met.**

### Stage 0 — Repository and harness


### Standing rules for the agent

1. **Never weaken a fidelity test to make a change pass.** If a change conflicts with the suite, the change is wrong or the spec needs amending — and amending the spec is a human decision, recorded in the exceptions register.
2. **Never phrase generated user-facing text as a recommendation.** Explain, never advise. This applies to tips, coaching, recaps, and every Codex entry.
3. **Never introduce a market-impact coefficient, a slippage formula, or a synthetic price generator.** Impact emerges from book depth. If impact looks wrong, the participant population is wrong.
4. **Never add an undo, rewind, or restore for a filled order or a busted account.** Permanence is load-bearing.
5. **Every engine change ships with a fidelity report** in the commit or PR description.
6. **Ledger operations are append-only and double-entry.** No destructive balance mutations.
